The Challenge

Water absorbs light.
AI restores it.

Water preferentially absorbs red and orange wavelengths within the first few meters of depth, producing the characteristic blue-green cast seen in underwater photography. Suspended particles scatter remaining light, reducing contrast and obscuring fine detail.

Conventional white-balance correction fails because the degradation is non-uniform and depth-dependent. GANs learn the full inverse transform end-to-end — mapping from degraded to clean without hand-crafted assumptions about the water column.

Capabilities

What the model learns

Spectral reconstruction

Recovers red and orange wavelengths absorbed at depth, restoring natural scene illumination without manual white-balancing.

Turbidity correction

Removes suspended particle scatter that reduces contrast and obscures detail — trained on real open-water degradation conditions.

U-Net skip connections

Encoder–decoder architecture with skip connections preserves fine structural detail through the full 256×256 spatial resolution.

< 2s per image

CPU inference on standard hardware. No GPU required at runtime. FastAPI backend handles concurrent requests with async I/O.

UIEB Benchmark

Trained and evaluated on 890 paired underwater images — the standard benchmark for underwater image enhancement research.

Supervised GAN

Ground-truth reference pairs enable L1 pixel supervision (λ=100) alongside adversarial training for photorealistic output.
